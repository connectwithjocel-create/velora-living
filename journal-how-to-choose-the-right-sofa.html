How to Choose the Right Sofa for Your Space

Measure first, sit second, choose fabric last — a practical guide to finding a sofa that fits your room and your routine.

A sofa is usually the largest, most-used piece of furniture in a home, and the hardest to return. Getting it right comes down to three things in a specific order: size, comfort and materials. Style matters, but a beautiful sofa that blocks a walkway or swallows its sitters won't stay loved for long.

1. Measure the room — and the route in

Start with the wall or zone the sofa will occupy. As a guideline, a sofa looks best at roughly two-thirds the length of the wall behind it, leaving space for side tables or lamps at each end.

  • Walkways: allow about 30–36 inches for main circulation paths.
  • Coffee table gap: 16–18 inches between sofa and table keeps drinks within reach without crowding knees.
  • Delivery route: measure doorways, hallways, stair turns and elevators. Compare them with the sofa's height and diagonal depth, not just its width.

Mark the footprint on the floor with painter's tape and live with it for a day. It's the simplest way to understand scale before you buy.

2. Understand seat depth and height

Seat depth — the distance from the front edge to the back cushion — shapes how a sofa feels more than any other dimension.

Seat depthFeels likeBest for
20–22 inUpright, supportiveConversation, shorter sitters, formal rooms
22–24 inBalancedMost living rooms and mixed households
24 in +LoungingTall sitters, curling up, media rooms

Seat height between 17 and 19 inches suits most people. Lower seats feel relaxed but are harder to stand up from; higher seats are easier for older family members.

3. Look beneath the upholstery

Construction details are rarely visible in a showroom, but they determine how a sofa ages:

  • Frame: kiln-dried hardwood resists warping better than softwood or particleboard.
  • Suspension: sinuous springs or webbing give consistent support; ask what's used.
  • Cushion fill: high-resilience foam holds its shape; feather wraps add softness but need regular plumping.

4. Choose fabric for your life, not the showroom

Linen is breathable and relaxed but creases. Velvet is rich and surprisingly durable but shows pressure marks. Performance fabrics resist stains, which matters with children, pets or light colors. Bouclé brings texture but can snag on claws.

Tip: request a fabric swatch and look at it in your room at different times of day. Color under daylight and evening lamps can look very different from a product photo.

5. Match the silhouette to the room

Low backs and slim arms keep smaller rooms feeling open. Deep, loose-cushion sofas suit family rooms. Modular sofas like the Soren Modular Sofa adapt if you move often; tailored designs like the Aurelia Linen Sofa work across many styles.
